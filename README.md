# WellWatch PA

Crowdsourced discovery and risk triage for Pennsylvania's orphaned and abandoned oil and gas wells.

Pennsylvania invented the oil industry at Titusville in 1859 and never recorded where most of the holes
went. Roughly **27,000 orphan and abandoned wells are documented**, with credible estimates of **100,000 to
560,000 more** that were never written down. They vent methane, leak brine into streams, and in some cases
fill basements with explosive gas. The Department of Environmental Protection has plugged about 400 in three
years against a **$114.6 million federal formula grant** — enough for a small fraction of the inventory.

WellWatch PA does two things about that:

1. Gives residents a way to report a well they found, from a phone, offline.
2. Ranks the resulting inventory so a fixed plugging budget closes as much risk as possible.

## Running it

`index.html` is a single self-contained page. No build step, no dependencies, no server required.

```bash
open index.html
```

Or serve the folder if you prefer a local origin:

```bash
python -m http.server 8000
```

The only external request is to Google Fonts; the page falls back to system fonts if that is blocked.

## What it does

**Triage queue** — every located well ranked by risk score, filterable by county and risk tier, plotted on a
projected map of Pennsylvania.

**Well detail** — the full score composition for any well, so a ranking can be argued with rather than
taken on faith.

**Field report** — drop a pin, answer four plain-language questions, watch the score compute live, and see
where the report lands in the queue.

**Plugging planner** — set a grant amount and compare two allocation strategies against it.

## The risk model

Six weighted factors, 100 points total. Deliberately transparent and hand-auditable rather than learned —
a scoring model that decides how public money gets spent should be explainable to the person it affects.

| Factor | Max | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Occupied structure proximity | 30 | Methane migration into basements is the acute life-safety hazard |
| Surface methane concentration | 25 | Measured ppm at the wellhead |
| Water resource proximity | 15 | Brine and hydrocarbon migration into streams and water wells |
| Age and record quality | 12 | Pre-1900 completions have no construction records |
| Sensitive site within 1,500 ft | 10 | Schools, daycares, hospitals |
| Wellbore physical condition | 8 | Open hole, deteriorated, or intact |

Tiers: **Critical** ≥ 70, **High** 50–69, **Moderate** 30–49, **Low** < 30.

## The planner

Allocation under a budget cap is a 0/1 knapsack problem. The planner solves it greedily two ways and shows
the difference:

- **Highest risk first** — the intuitive approach. Deep, expensive wells consume the grant early.
- **Risk retired per dollar** — rank by `score / cost`.

At most budgets the second strategy closes meaningfully more wells and retires more total risk for the same
money. That comparison is the actual argument of the project: the ranking is not decoration, it changes what
the grant buys.

## Data

> **The well data in this prototype is synthetic.**

Locations, depths, methane readings and plugging costs are generated by a seeded PRNG, not measured. They
are shaped to match the documented distribution of Pennsylvania's legacy wells — concentrated in the
Venango–Warren–McKean oil region — so the demonstration is realistic, but **no individual well on this map
is a real well, and nothing here should be used to make a real siting or safety decision.**

A production deployment would replace the generator with:

- **DEP Oil & Gas Locations** — seed inventory
- **USGS National Hydrography Dataset** — stream distances
- **Microsoft US Building Footprints** — structure distances
- **NCES school locations** — sensitive sites

and write reports back through the DEP eFACTS intake.

## Intended production architecture

The prototype runs entirely client-side. The design it stands in for:

- **Client** — React + Vite PWA, offline-first. IndexedDB queue and Background Sync, because oil country has
  no cell coverage. MediaDevices and Geolocation for capture.
- **API** — FastAPI (Python). `POST /reports`, `GET /wells`, `GET /plan`, `GET /export.csv`.
- **Domain services** — versioned and pure, so a rubric change re-scores history: `score_v1`, `enrich`,
  `dedupe`, `allocate`.
- **Storage** — PostgreSQL + PostGIS with a GiST index for radius queries; Cloudflare R2 for field photos.

Server-side enrichment matters: a reporter's distance estimate is replaced by a real PostGIS measurement
against building footprints and NHD flowlines. Duplicate reports are merged within a 60 m radius so two
people photographing opposite sides of the same wellhead produce one well, not two.
