The Problem

Pennsylvania drilled the world's first commercial oil well at Titusville in 1859, then spent the next century not writing down where the holes went. Those wells are still here. They vent methane, leak brine into streams, and occasionally fill basements with explosive gas.

27,000 documented 100,000+ unrecorded 400 plugged in 3 years $114.6M available

How Pennsylvania Got Here

1859

Edwin Drake drills at Titusville. The commercial oil industry starts in Venango County.

1900s

Tens of thousands of wells are drilled with no requirement to record their locations.

1984

The Oil and Gas Act finally requires registration. Everything drilled earlier stays invisible.

2021

Federal infrastructure money arrives for plugging, but it covers a fraction of the inventory.

    How to Report a Well

    Before You Go Near It

    • Do not smoke or run an engine beside an open wellbore
    • Stay upwind if you can smell gas
    • Never lower anything into the hole
    • Keep children and animals back from the opening

    What to Record

    • A GPS fix, with accuracy better than 30 feet
    • Photos of the pipe and the ground around it
    • Distance to the nearest building and stream
    • Anything you can smell or hear

    Who to Tell

    • PA DEP regional office for your county
    • DEP's oil and gas complaint line for active leaks
    • 911 if gas is entering a building
    • Your county conservation district

    Why It Adds Up

    • An unlocated well cannot be scheduled for plugging
    • Reports build the record the grant money is spent against
    • One confirmed location can move a whole queue
